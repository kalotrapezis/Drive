What Tetra does for you

Files to and from your PC in a second

Share anything to Send to computer on the phone, or Send to phone on the PC. It goes over your own Wi-Fi: no cable, no cloud. If the computer is away, it goes when it is back.

Call from your computer

Click a number in Contacts on the PC and your phone dials it. A call bubble on the screen answers, holds, mutes, hangs up and moves the sound to the phone's loudspeaker.

Maps of any country, offline

Pick a country on the computer: Tetra makes its map, search and routes from OpenStreetMap and sends them to your phone and tablet. Routes cross borders at the real crossings, with directions in your language.

In your language, with a guide

All 24 official languages of the EU. A guide on first start, and any time in Settings, shows where to tap and why, step by step.

Your data plan, watched

Tell Tetra your plan once. It notifies you at 50 %, 80 % and 100 %, or at any steps you choose, and forecasts the end of the month before you get there.

Calendar and timers, and AI if you want it

Built-in skills let Claude, ChatGPT or any assistant that can use your computer import a timetable, plan your week or start a timer. They simply edit the calendar's and timers' files. AI Assist, off until you switch it on, can do the same inside Tetra.

Notes and lists, organised

Keep your notes and shopping lists organised with tags and in sync between the phone and the computer. Each note is a plain file in your own Tetra folder.

Photos that know who and where

Tags, live photos, and People and Documents collections from face recognition that runs on the device. Find photos by place, and bring your Google Photos over (Takeout import on the computer).

Scan any page clean

Filters and automatic cropping find and straighten the page: all the tools you need to make a perfect digital copy, for as many pages as you like.

Hide what should stay private

Redact PDFs and photos so the hidden part is removed from the file itself, not just covered. Tetra finds the eyes in a photo and covers them with black bars or stars, and finds text to hide.

Office and PDF, good enough

Word, Excel and PowerPoint open and save as themselves, compatible enough for everyday work. PDFs are edited the way Words edits, and films and sounds inside documents play. A phone layout you can actually use.

Keep the internal SSD light

Pair an external drive with Tetra and it backs everything up automatically, or moves older photos onto it so the computer's own disk stays as empty as possible.

More than one copy, no subscription

Tetra shows how many places hold each file, the phone, the tablet, the computer or a USB drive, and what still lives in only one. Your own backups, with no cloud plan to pay for.

Mail, kept on this computer

Every mail and attachment is saved as an ordinary file: green when it is saved here, orange when it is only on the server. Storage finds even the mail you deleted from the server. Gmail, school and Microsoft accounts work. A phone shows one pane at a time; a wide tablet shows folders, list and mail side by side.

AI Assist, off until you switch it on

Ask about your mail, calendar, timers, notes, files, contacts, maps or the web, with a Mistral key you paste. One switch turns all AI off, and then nothing is sent anywhere. A switch per app decides what it may see.

Each device, said in plain words

Every phone, tablet and drive card says what it does, one sentence per app. Edit opens a guide: which app, Off, Mirror or Move, how much stays on the device, a map of what moves, then Confirm. On a phone or tablet, photos move by default and the rest is copied. At 90 % full, Tetra suggests what to move, and moves nothing without asking.

Every applet, on every screen

One Home, one folder, the same applets on Android and Linux. Also inside: Mail, AI Assist, Scanner, Codes and a Text editor.

Questions

How do I install Tetra from outside the Play Store?
  1. On your phone, open this page or scan the QR code, and tap Download for Android.
  2. When the download ends, tap its notification, or open Files › Downloads and tap Tetra-…-arm64.apk.
  3. The first time, Android says your browser (or Files) is not allowed to install apps. Tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, and go back.
  4. Tap Install. If Play Protect warns about an unknown app, tap More details, then Install anyway.
  5. Tap Open. Tetra's guide starts and shows you around.

Each phone maker words these steps a little differently, but the steps are the same. Updates install the same way, over the old version, and keep everything.

Is it safe to install an APK like this?

Download Tetra only from this page or from its GitHub releases. Every release is signed with the same key, and Android installs an update only if it carries that signature. The source code is public for anyone to read.

How do I start the AppImage on Linux?

Download it, right-click it › Properties › Permissions › Allow executing file as program (or run chmod +x Tetra-*.AppImage), then double-click it. On Debian, Ubuntu and Mint the .deb opens in your software installer, or installs with sudo apt install ./local-drive-desktop_*_amd64.deb.

Do I need a computer?

No. Tetra works alone on a phone or a tablet, mail included: the phone fetches your accounts itself. With the desktop app your files sync over your own Wi-Fi, the computer makes the maps of any country, and it keeps the full copy of your mail.

Does it cost anything, or need an account?

No. Tetra is free software (GPL 3.0): no account, no ads, no tracking. Your files stay on your devices. Mail goes only between your devices and your own mail provider, and AI Assist talks to Mistral only if you switch it on and ask it something.

Where are my files?

In one folder: Tetra on the phone's storage and ~/Tetra on the computer. They are plain files, photos, documents, notes, calendars (.ics), contacts (.vcf) and mail (.eml, in .System/Mail), that other apps can open too.

Which mail accounts work?

Gmail with an app password: Google asks for one instead of your usual password, and Tetra shows you where to make it. School accounts such as sch.gr with their password. Hotmail, Outlook and Microsoft 365 sign in with Microsoft, but first each needs a one-time app registration with Microsoft, which is free: you make it once and enter its Application (client) ID in Settings › Mail. A university may need its IT department to approve the app first. The steps are in MAIL_PLAN.md in the Tetra-mobile repository. An account that the phone cannot fetch itself shows through this computer, marked ⇄.

Does Tetra delete my mail?

Only what you say so, and only after a check. Each account starts with an archive check that shows what would be archived, deleted on the server and deleted here, with counts. Nothing is cleaned until you press Run; after that the clean-up runs once a day. The server only loses mail that is already saved on this computer.

Can Tetra be my default email app?

Yes. In Settings › Mail, Default email app makes Tetra the app that opens email links (mailto:), so a link on a web page starts a new mail in Tetra. Phones can do the same.

Is AI on? What leaves my computer?

AI is off on a new install: AI Assist is hidden and nothing is sent to any AI service. To use it, turn it on in Settings › AI Assist and paste your own Mistral key, made at console.mistral.ai. What you say and ask, and the short answers the tools give, go to Mistral; your files stay here. A switch per app decides what it may read: mail, calendar, notes, files, contacts, phone, timers, maps and the web. With the switch off, nothing at all is sent.

What can I ask AI Assist to do?
  1. New mail, the mail that needs an answer, and what matters most: deadlines, payments, appointments.
  2. Find any mail by sender, subject, words or date, even one deleted from the server.
  3. Copy a mail or an attachment to Documents.
  4. Write a mail or a reply. It shows you the draft, and sends it only when you press Send or say yes.
  5. Calendar and timers: add an event, see what is on a day or when you are free; start, list or stop a timer.
  6. Notes, shopping lists and files.
  7. Contacts, a call on your phone once you confirm, and a route in Maps.
  8. A web search, a YouTube search, or a picture.
